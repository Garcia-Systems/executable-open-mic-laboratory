# Research Guidelines for Contributors

## Citation standards

- Use APA 7 formatting in `docs/references.md`.
- Prefer peer-reviewed literature, academic books, university press books, recognized pedagogy texts, AES or professional audio references, and established engineering references.
- Include DOIs when they are available and stable URLs only when they are necessary.
- Do not cite blogs, marketing pages, or unsupported internet claims as evidence for educational claims.

## Distinguishing evidence from heuristics

When adding a feature, label its claims with the repository vocabulary:

- **Research Finding** for supported scholarly claims.
- **Professional Practice** for field conventions.
- **Engineering Principle** for technical design principles.
- **Educational Heuristic** for repository-designed formulas, thresholds, scores, and recommendations.
- **Scenario Assumption** for fictional scenario details.
- **Reflection Prompt** for learner questions.
- **Subjective Artistic Judgment** for taste, meaning, identity, or interpretive choices.

## Avoiding overclaiming

Do not write that research proves a repository score, threshold, rank, or recommendation. Prefer: “Research motivates this concept; the deterministic implementation is an original educational model.” If evidence is mixed, say so.

## Documenting scoring engines

Every scoring or recommendation engine should document:

- Purpose
- Inputs
- Outputs
- Evidence Basis
- Repository Contribution
- Limitations
- Non-predictive nature

## Updating chapters

When adding or changing a chapter, include a short `Research Foundations` section near the beginning and a `References and Further Reading` section near the end. Keep citations readable and avoid turning learner chapters into literature reviews.
