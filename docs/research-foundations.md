# Research Foundations

Executable Open Mic Laboratory is an evidence-informed executable textbook. It uses research and professional practice to motivate learning concepts, then implements transparent deterministic simulations as original educational models.

## Evidence labels

- **Research Finding**: A concept supported by peer-reviewed research, academic books, or established scholarly synthesis.
- **Professional Practice**: A field convention used by performers, teachers, stage crews, or audio engineers.
- **Engineering Principle**: A technical principle from audio, software, or systems practice.
- **Educational Heuristic**: A repository-designed simplification intended to make learning inspectable and repeatable.
- **Scenario Assumption**: A fictional or bounded assumption used to make a laboratory scenario runnable.
- **Reflection Prompt**: A learner-facing question intended to support metacognition rather than produce a measurement.
- **Subjective Artistic Judgment**: A value judgment about meaning, taste, identity, emotional effect, or artistic priority.

## What comes from existing literature

The repository draws on music education, expertise, deliberate practice, self-regulated learning, cognitive load, feedback, reflection, music cognition, audience experience, improvisation, experiential learning, and simulation-based education. These traditions support broad concepts such as focused practice, feedback loops, metacognitive planning, attention limits, adaptation under constraints, and reflection after performance.

## What comes from professional practice

The chapters on repertoire management, programming, stagecraft, open-mic etiquette, soundcheck, gain structure, monitors, microphones, and recovery reflect common professional practices. These practices are presented as useful conventions, not universal rules.

## What comes from engineering standards

The software favors deterministic functions, typed dataclasses, visible inputs and outputs, repeatable CLI demonstrations, and tests. Audio chapters distinguish signal-flow and gain-structure principles from the repository's venue scenarios.

## What is original to this repository

The exact readiness, suitability, repertoire-health, coordination, audience-response, communication, recovery, improvisation, originality, and improvement scores are original educational heuristics. They are designed to compare scenarios, reveal tradeoffs, and support discussion. They are not validated measurement instruments.

## Why deterministic simulations simplify education

Deterministic simulations let learners rerun an experiment and see the same result. That repeatability is useful for debugging reasoning, comparing alternatives, and understanding cause-and-effect assumptions. The tradeoff is simplification: human performance, audience response, venue acoustics, and artistic meaning are more variable than a deterministic model can represent.

## Limitations

- Scores are educational comparison tools, not predictions of success.
- The repository does not diagnose vocal health, anxiety, learning disability, hearing safety, or equipment safety.
- Audience and artistry models are especially limited because perception, culture, identity, and context vary widely.
- Audio recommendations cannot replace venue-specific listening, measurement, and qualified technical judgment.
- Where evidence is mixed, the repository names the uncertainty rather than converting it into a false rule.
